# Related work

The trading result is not a claim that a published network already makes money on this contract. The method is assembled from four groups of work, and the PO3/IFVG overlay is a hypothesis those papers do not test.

## Reward and optimizer

Moody and Saffell ([NeurIPS 1998](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1998/file/4e6cd95227cb0c280e99a195be5f6615-Paper.pdf)) optimize trading-system objectives including profit, Sharpe, and the online differential Sharpe ratio. A profit objective and a Sharpe objective can induce different positions. That is the objective contrast in this thesis: Idea 3 uses differential Sharpe (`DSRReward`), while Ideas 1 and 2 use trade PnL. The optimizer is proximal policy optimization (PPO; [Schulman et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)), implemented through Stable-Baselines3. PPO is the fixed algorithm in this experiment, not a claim that PPO is best for this market or a published Nasdaq result.

## Trading literature

Zhang, Zohren, and Roberts ([Journal of Financial Data Science, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3905/jfds.2020.1.030)) study DRL on 50 liquid continuous futures from 2011 to 2019, considering discrete and continuous actions with volatility scaling. They compare with time-series momentum ([Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003)), a futures strategy whose paper studies persistence over one-to-12-month horizons. This is contextual precedent for algorithmic trading, not a result for this cash-index M1 environment or its structural rules. Idea 3 is related to their discrete-action family but uses PPO rather than their algorithms and has different instruments, observations, costs, and execution semantics. The published performance table is not a transferable benchmark; this thesis uses its own data and evaluation lineage.

FinRL ([Liu et al., ICAIF 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09395)) presents a DRL framework and trading examples. Yang et al. ([ICAIF 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3383455.3422540)) compare PPO, A2C, and DDPG and an ensemble on 30 Dow Jones stocks using daily data. These works establish precedent for applying DRL to trading; they do not establish PPO's superiority for this thesis. PPO is selected here as a fixed, supported on-policy method so the thesis can test the feature/eligibility question. No weights from those studies are used. Time-ordered train/test separation is a general evaluation requirement, not a contribution inherited uniquely from FinRL.

Jiang, Xu, and Liang ([2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059)) map price-history tensors to portfolio weights in a cryptocurrency portfolio task. Bai, Kolter, and Koltun ([2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271)) study temporal convolutions on sequence-modeling tasks; that motivates comparing a TCN with a GRU ([Cho et al., 2014](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1179/)) and Transformer ([Vaswani et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)), but does not establish trading performance. These papers are architecture or portfolio-method context, not intraday execution baselines.

## Holdout, selection, and the kill switch

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, and Zhu ([Journal of Computational Finance, 2017](https://doi.org/10.21314/jcf.2016.322)) define a framework for estimating backtest overfitting when selecting among trials. Bailey and López de Prado ([Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2460551)) define the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, accounting for selection across multiple trials and non-normal returns. The six architectures and two strategy modes are part of the experiment's trial history and must remain visible. Purging is used to reduce overlap-related leakage between training and test folds ([López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, 2018, ch. 7](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/advances-in-financial/9781119482086/c07.xhtml)); it does not repair feature lookahead within a fold. The 2026 slice remains locked for selection and reporting under the validation protocol.

Achiam et al. ([ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/achiam17a.html)) introduce Constrained Policy Optimization with near-constraint-satisfaction guarantees during policy search. Ray, Achiam, and Amodei ([2019](https://openai.com/index/benchmarking-safe-exploration-in-deep-reinforcement-learning/)) formulate and benchmark constrained safe-exploration methods and introduce Safety Gym. This thesis instead enforces daily-loss, max-loss, and trailing-drawdown rules in the environment, including episode termination; that is a simulator-side risk gate, not constrained policy optimization or a guarantee of safe exploration. Huang and Ontañón ([FLAIRS 2022](https://doi.org/10.32473/flairs.v35i.130584)) analyze state-dependent invalid-action masks for policy-gradient algorithms. Their result motivates careful gate semantics but does not establish correctness or benefit for this thesis's PO3/IFVG eligibility rule. Sutton, Precup, and Singh's ([1999 options framework](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(99)00052-1)) options are temporally extended closed-loop policies. The thesis's stop, risk, and reward-to-risk action coordinates are management parameters, not formal options.

## What the overlay is not

Lo, Mamaysky, and Wang ([Journal of Finance, 2000](https://www.mit.edu/~wangj/pap/LoMamayskyWang00.pdf)) test systematic detection of selected chart patterns in daily US-stock data. They do not test this thesis's power-of-three or inverse fair-value-gap definitions. This thesis treats those definitions as a hypothesis to evaluate; the related work cited here does not validate their performance. Idea 2's "distribution" is a price-delivery phase, not distributional reinforcement learning.

## Rule-based comparators

The project also implements flat cash, buy-and-hold, EMA/MACD/RSI, and previous-session-return comparators. These are implementation-defined baselines, not published evidence for the PO3/IFVG overlay. The previous-session sign rule is a one-session adaptation of time-series momentum, not a replication of the one-to-12-month futures strategy studied by Moskowitz et al. The cash-hold sizing, swap, and margin assumptions are specified in [`cash_hold.py`](../../trading/baselines/cash_hold.py) and scored by [`score_rule_baselines.py`](../../scripts/research/thesis/evaluation/score_rule_baselines.py); they remain simulator assumptions pending resolution of the feed and contract provenance gate.
